Rescue Process Updated Nov 2026

What "Rescue Only" (New Hope and Transfer Preferred) Means at CA Shelters

When a dog is marked Rescue Only, New Hope, or Transfer Preferred at shelters like Devore, Riverside, LA Animal Services, or Contra Costa, the public cannot walk in and adopt that dog directly. The dog has to be pulled by an approved nonprofit rescue group.

How you can still help: Most rescue groups want to save more dogs, but they do not have empty foster homes. If you can foster the dog for 2 to 4 weeks, message local rescue groups with the dog's exact Animal ID, shelter name, and details about your home so they can place a rescue hold in time.

Urgent Steps Updated Nov 2026

How to Move Fast When a Shelter Dog Has a 24 to 72 Hour Deadline

  • Always start with the Animal ID: Shelter staff look up dogs by ID (such as A1261085), not by name, because several dogs often share the same name.
  • Check the dog's current status: Open the shelter link to confirm whether the dog is open for public adoption or needs a rescue group transfer.
  • Mention if you can isolate a sick dog: For medical cases like kennel cough, letting a rescue know you can keep the dog separate from your own pets for 14 days makes approval much faster.

California Shelter Urgent Lists Covered on UrgentPaws

UrgentPaws tracks urgent shelter lists across three parts of California: the Bay Area (Oakland Animal Services Rescue Needed, San Jose Animal Care Center Rescue Priority, and Contra Costa Transfer Preferred), the Central Valley (Stanislaus County RED and Final Call, and Kern County Animals at Greatest Risk), and Southern California (LA Animal Services Euthanasia, Urgent, and Medical Alerts, Riverside County Red List and Rescue Only, and San Bernardino Devore).
